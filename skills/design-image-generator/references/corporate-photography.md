# Corporate and editorial photography

Use this reference for permitted corporate, professional, office, urban,
mobility, and human-centred editorial concepts. The imagery guideline remains
the authority for brand fit and AI eligibility.

## Visual objective

The result should feel credible, natural, composed, and editorial. It should not
look like generic corporate stock, luxury advertising, a travel campaign, or a
synthetic executive portrait.

Build the scene before adding style language:

- who or what is shown;
- what is happening;
- why the environment is relevant;
- where the viewer's attention should land;
- what space the final layout needs for copy;
- what emotional signal the image should communicate.

## People boundary

Synthetic people are permitted only in otherwise eligible concepts,
prototypes, or non-premium use. They are not real representatives of Global
Citizen Solutions.

Never present a synthetic person as:

- an advisor, employee, client, speaker, partner, or testimonial author;
- a recognisable or named individual;
- evidence of a real service outcome;
- a real participant in a case study or event.

When identity matters, use a real supplied photograph and the image tool's
supported reference/edit flow. Do not reconstruct the person from prose.

## Composition

- Prefer close or mid framing for individual human scenes when the placement
  needs emotional connection.
- Use environmental framing when context is necessary to explain the work,
  jurisdiction, or mobility situation.
- Keep faces and eyes naturally sharp without making the background look
  artificially erased.
- Leave deliberate negative space only where the final layout needs it.
- Avoid staged pointing, handshakes, arms-crossed executive poses, laptop
  demonstrations, celebratory gestures, or everyone looking at the camera.
- Keep desks, architecture, transit areas, and city backgrounds clean but
  believable; avoid sterile or impossible environments.

## Camera guidance by scene

Camera language is a creative cue, not a provider command.

| Scene | Useful starting point | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Close individual portrait | 70–105 mm portrait look, moderate shallow focus | Extreme blur, beauty-editorial compression |
| Mid-shot professional | 50–85 mm, enough depth to retain context | Artificial cut-out subject |
| Environmental portrait | 35–50 mm, balanced subject and setting | Distorted wide-angle face |
| Small group or family | 35–50 mm, sufficient depth for every face | One sharp face with others blurred |
| Architecture or skyline | 24–35 mm, deeper focus and controlled verticals | 85 mm portrait treatment, warped buildings |
| Interior | 28–50 mm depending on room size | Unrealistically vast rooms or stretched furniture |

Do not append “85 mm, f/1.8, shallow depth of field” to every prompt. Select the
lens look and depth from the scene's needs.

## Lighting and grade

- Soft natural directional light.
- Controlled highlights and clean shadows.
- Warm-neutral, muted, filmic colour treatment.
- Subtle organic contrast and film grain.
- Natural skin and fabric texture.
- No beauty retouching or artificial skin smoothing.
- No tropical golden-hour saturation, HDR, neon colour casts, or dramatic
  cinematic lighting.

## Expression and wardrobe

- Calm, attentive, thoughtful, or approachable expressions.
- A small natural smile is acceptable; avoid exaggerated teeth-forward smiles.
- Wardrobe should be contemporary, understated, and appropriate to the setting.
- Avoid status signalling through luxury watches, designer logos, cars, yachts,
  or conspicuously expensive accessories.
- Represent age, ethnicity, gender, disability, and family composition
  intentionally when specified. When not specified, choose a credible,
  non-stereotyped subject.

## Environment

Useful environments:

- understated legal or advisory office;
- calm boardroom or meeting space;
- city or financial district with restrained background activity;
- residential setting for a considered family conversation;
- institutional architecture;
- airport or transit environment only when mobility is materially relevant.

Avoid:

- airport lounge glamour, first-class signalling, luggage advertising, or
  holiday departure energy;
- resorts, beaches, yachts, luxury cars, penthouses, and private jets;
- prominent flags, political settings, immigration counters, passport closeups,
  or security-screening scenes;
- visible invented company names, logos, badges, documents, or screen text.

## Prompt construction

Use this order:

~~~text
Subject and credible action
Environment and why it matters
Composition, crop, viewpoint, and negative space
Scene-appropriate lens look and depth of field
Soft natural directional lighting
Warm-neutral muted filmic grade
Realistic skin, fabric, material, or architectural texture
Relevant exclusions
Aspect ratio and intended placement
~~~

Example for an eligible internal concept:

~~~text
Editorial documentary photograph of a fictional international entrepreneur in
a quiet contemporary office, reviewing printed planning notes beside a window,
composed and attentive rather than posed, mid-shot environmental portrait with
balanced office context and clean negative space on the left, natural 50 mm
look with moderate depth of field, soft directional daylight, warm-neutral
muted filmic colour grade, realistic skin and fabric texture, subtle grain, no
visible logos or screen text, no handshake, no exaggerated smile, no luxury
status symbols, no artificial skin smoothing, 3:2 landscape for an internal
campaign concept.
~~~

Adapt the example. Do not reuse its fictional subject or 3:2 ratio when the
brief requires something else.

## QA for human scenes

- Eyes, teeth, ears, hands, fingers, jewellery, eyewear, and phone edges are
  anatomically plausible.
- Skin retains pores and age-appropriate texture.
- Clothing folds and patterns remain coherent.
- Group members interact naturally and share consistent lighting.
- Background people do not contain distorted faces or bodies.
- Reflections and shadows match the scene.
- Any device, paper, signage, or badge is free from accidental text.
- The subject is not implied to be a real GCS representative.
