The Map We Redrew
Alongside The World
2016
2016

Brexit changes the way Europe thinks about mobility.

Millions of people begin to realise that rights once considered permanent, including freedom of movement, can also change with political decisions.

  • Global Citizen Solutions is born.
2017
2017

Investment migration gains momentum worldwide.

New residency and citizenship programmes expand the possibilities available to investors and families looking for greater freedom, security and international opportunities.

  • Global mobility begins to reach a wider audience.
2018
2018

Citizenship by investment becomes more accessible and competitive.

Turkey significantly reduces its minimum real estate investment requirement, attracting a new generation of international investors.

  • Investment migration moves further into the mainstream.
2019
2019

New global mobility hubs begin to emerge.

While Europe increases its scrutiny of Golden Visa and citizenship programmes, the UAE launches its Golden Visa for investors, professionals and exceptional talent.

  • Global mobility expands beyond its traditional markets.
2020
2020

Global mobility takes on a new meaning.

Remote work accelerates, new ways of living across borders begin to emerge, and demand grows for alternative residency, citizenship and international planning options.

  • The freedom to choose where to live becomes more valuable than ever.
2021
2021

The world reopens, but the way people live and work has changed.

Remote work continues to accelerate demand for digital nomad visas, alternative residencies and new possibilities for living outside one’s country of origin.

  • At the same time, Brexit brings an end to freedom of movement between the UK and the European Union.
  • Living and working no longer have to happen in the same country.
2022
2022

Global mobility becomes increasingly connected to security, opportunity and long-term planning.

Major geopolitical events reinforce the importance of having options across borders, while governments and families place greater focus on residency, citizenship and international mobility strategies.

  • For families around the world, having options becomes more important than ever.
2023
2023

Investment migration enters a new phase.

Portugal reshapes its Golden Visa, while the market becomes more diversified, with new investment structures and a broader approach to residency, wealth and family planning.

  • GCS grows with the transformation of global mobility.
2024
2024

Portugal becomes one of the decade’s leading international mobility destinations.

More than 1.5 million foreign citizens are living in Portugal, compared with fewer than 400,000 in 2016.

  • Across Europe, investment migration continues to evolve as countries reassess traditional real estate-led Golden Visa models.
  • A new generation of global citizens is choosing where and how they want to live.
2025
2025

Investment migration continues to evolve.

Spain closes its Golden Visa, Portugal has already moved away from direct real estate investment, and Greece introduces higher investment thresholds in its most sought-after regions.

  • At the same time, the sector becomes increasingly focused on regulation, transparency and long-term value.
  • Global mobility becomes more sophisticated, diverse and strategic.
2026
2026

Global mobility enters a new era.

Digital borders, biometric systems and evolving residency and citizenship frameworks are reshaping how people move between countries.

  • Ten years after GCS was founded, the conversation is no longer simply about obtaining a visa or a second passport.
  • It is about where to live, where to invest, how to protect a family’s future and how to preserve freedom of choice in a changing world.
